## Operative provisions

**Definition (Sec. 2).** A "payment stablecoin" is a digital asset designed for
payment/settlement, redeemable by its issuer for a fixed monetary value, and
represented as maintaining stable value. Excludes central bank money, bank deposits
(incl. tokenized deposits), and securities.

**Reserves (Sec. 4).** ≥1:1 backing in: US currency; insured demand deposits;
**US Treasuries with remaining maturity ≤93 days**; Treasury-backed repos (≤7 days)
/ overnight reverse repos; government money market funds; central bank reserves;
tokenized equivalents. No rehypothecation except narrow purposes. Monthly public
reserve reports examined by a registered accounting firm, certified by CEO/CFO under
criminal penalty.

**Yield ban (Sec. 4(a)(11)).** No issuer may pay holders "any form of interest or
yield … solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention" of the stablecoin.
Notably does not expressly reach third-party (exchange/affiliate) rewards — the
"three-party model" loophole the OCC's 2026 proposal targets.

**Three licensing paths.** (1) Subsidiary of an insured depository institution;
(2) OCC-supervised **federal qualified nonbank issuer**; (3) **state qualified
issuer** — capped at **$10B outstanding**, in states certified "substantially
similar"; crossing $10B forces transition to federal oversight within 360 days.

**Not a security, not a commodity (Sec. 17).** Removes SEC/CFTC jurisdiction over
compliant payment stablecoins; also not insured deposits, and marketing may not
imply federal backing or legal-tender status.

**Big Tech gate.** A public company not predominantly engaged in financial
activities may issue only with a **unanimous vote of the Stablecoin Certification
Review Committee** (Treasury Secretary chair, Fed, FDIC), plus data-use and privacy
conditions.

**Foreign issuers (Sec. 18).** Tether-style issuers may serve the US market only if
Treasury determines their home regime is **comparable**, they **register with the
OCC**, hold **US-based reserves sufficient for US-customer demand**, can honor
lawful freeze/seize orders, and are not domiciled in sanctioned/AML-concern
jurisdictions. Treasury may negotiate **reciprocal arrangements** with comparable
jurisdictions.

**AML.** Issuers are BSA "financial institutions": full AML/CFT/sanctions programs
and technical capability to freeze/burn/block on lawful order (FinCEN + OFAC joint
NPRM, April 2026 — the first US rule mandating a formal sanctions-compliance
program for a class of US persons).

**Insolvency (Sec. 11).** Reserves excluded from the bankruptcy estate; holders take
priority over all other claims on reserves (workability contested — see
interpretations brief).

**Effective date (Sec. 20).** Earlier of **January 18, 2027** (18 months after
enactment) or 120 days after final implementing regulations. As of July 2026 no
final rules were out, so January 18, 2027 governs; the DASP secondary-market cutoff
is July 18, 2028.
