# GENIUS Act — Accredited Interpretations & Implementation Brief

> Research brief compiled 2026-07-07 from web-search results (titles/snippets/summaries;
> direct page fetches unavailable in this environment). Low-confidence items flagged inline.

## Law firm consensus

The Act imposes "bank-like regulation and supervision" on all issuers regardless of
charter (Davis Polk); creates dual federal/state licensing with strong federal gravity
above $10B (Latham, Skadden); the non-security/non-commodity carve-out is the single
biggest legal-clarity win (Latham, Skadden). Open issues: the third-party yield
loophole, treatment of DeFi, and insolvency workability.

## Notable interpretive debates

1. **The yield loophole.** Sec. 4(a)(11) bars issuers from paying interest "solely in
   connection with holding" — but not third parties. Coinbase's USDC rewards became
   the test case (CLS Blue Sky, Dec 2025). BPI/banking trades lobby to close it; the
   OCC's 2026 proposal restricts indirect yield via affiliates/distribution partners;
   Cato argues against.
2. **Insolvency workability.** Reserves sit outside the estate with holder
   super-priority; Morgan Lewis / Squire Patton Boggs note Chapter 11 may be
   unworkable (no estate funding); Levitin's "fifth priority" critique contested.
3. **Foreign-issuer comparability (Sec. 18).** Treasury's comparability/reciprocity
   rule still unissued as of mid-2026 — the single biggest open question for Tether's
   US access (codified at 12 U.S.C. 5916).

## Currency-dominance signals in the US record

- **Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent** (enactment statement, sb0197): the Act "will
  buttress the dollar's status as the global reserve currency, expand access to the
  dollar economy for billions across the globe, and lead to a surge in demand for US
  Treasuries, which back stablecoins."
- Bessent (June 2025): market "could exceed $2 trillion by 2028"; on X: "$3.7 trillion
  by the end of the decade… stablecoins can reinforce dollar supremacy."
- **David Sacks** (White House crypto czar): the Act "extends U.S. dollar dominance online."
- **Citi GPS "Stablecoins 2030"**: $1.6T base / $3.7T bull by 2030 (later $1.9T/$4.0T);
  issuers could hold ~$1.2T of US government debt by 2030 — potentially exceeding
  every foreign sovereign holder. TBAC flagged stablecoins as a major new UST demand source.
- Academic framing: Columbia Economic Review, "Digitalizing Dominance: How the GENIUS
  Act Reinforces US Dollar Hegemony."
