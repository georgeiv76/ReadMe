# The Currency-Dominance Dimension of Stablecoin Regulation — Research Brief

> Compiled 2026-07-07 from web-search results (titles/snippets/summaries; direct page
> fetches unavailable in this environment). Conflicting figures flagged inline.

## 1. The dollar side: GENIUS as dollar-extension machinery

**USD dominance.** USD-pegged stablecoins are **~97-99%** of the total market
(~$290-320B mid-2026; trackers disagree by ~$30B). USDT ~$186B (~59%) + USDC ~$75B
(~24%) ≈ 83% together. Trajectory: ~$173B (Sept 2024) → ~$205B (Jan 2025) → ~$306B
(end-Nov 2025, +49% YoY).

**Issuers as Treasury holders.** Tether reported **~$122B in T-bills (Dec 31, 2025
attestation; >$135-141B total UST exposure claimed)** — the **17th-largest holder of
US Treasuries, ahead of Germany (~$110B)**. Caveat: BDO attestations, never a full
Big-Four audit. Bo Hines (CEO, Tether's US arm USAT): Tether will be a top-10 T-bill
buyer in 2026. Circle holds ~$48B.

**Bessent's framing.** Enactment statement: stablecoins "will buttress the dollar's
status as the global reserve currency… and lead to a surge in demand for US
Treasuries." June 2025: "$3.7 trillion market by the end of the decade";
"stablecoins can reinforce dollar supremacy"; Nov 2025 (Politico Pro): expects a
$2-3T market becoming a significant buyer of government securities; crypto could
"lock in dollar supremacy."

**Projections.** Standard Chartered: $2T by end-2028 ≈ **$1.6T of additional T-bill
purchases** (~all planned net new T-bill issuance in the period). TBAC "Digital
Money" presentation (Apr 30, 2025): ~$2T by 2028 scenario. Citi: $1.6T base /
$2.9-3.7T bull by 2030; issuers could hold **~$1.2T of US government debt by 2030 —
potentially more than any foreign sovereign**. JPMorgan (skeptical): ~$500B by 2028.

**The captive-buyer mechanism.** GENIUS's reserve menu (≤93-day T-bills, overnight
repos, government MMFs) hardwires every dollar of global stablecoin growth into
short-dated US debt — "deliberate monetary policy by other means," "a new
distribution channel for American sovereign debt" (Columbia Economic Review; GIS
Reports). The **eurodollar analogy** ("Eurodollars 2.0," BIS WP 1146 "On par"):
offshore dollar claims historically entrenched dollar dominance; network effects
mean the first credible regulated digital-dollar rail captures payments globally.

**EM dollarization.** Goldman Sachs: ~66% of stablecoin supply held in emerging
markets. Argentina: stablecoins >50% of exchange volume, ~$34B transacted in 2024.
Nigeria: USDC volume +412% YoY, >$3B/month (secondhand BCG citation — flagged).
Castle Island/Brevan Howard survey: **47% of EM users hold stablecoins primarily
"to access dollars."**

## 2. The euro side: defensive sovereignty

- **Cipollone** (ECON, Apr 2025): dollar stablecoins could move euro deposits to the
  US, hand payment fees and data to US providers, and "compromise monetary
  sovereignty"; the digital euro would "prevent foreign-currency stablecoins from
  becoming widely used in the euro area."
- **Lagarde**: "privatization of money — I regard money as a public good" (Jul 2025);
  Tether/Circle dominance risks "**digital dollarisation**" of Europe (May 2026).
- **ECB to finance ministers (May 22, 2026):** stablecoin expansion could drain
  retail deposits, raise bank funding costs, impair monetary transmission; ECB WP
  3199 finds measurable deposit substitution already.
- **Euro stablecoins ≈ €450M total (~0.15% of market)** vs. digital euro as the
  strategic answer: ECB closed the preparation phase **Oct 29-30, 2025** and moved to
  build-out; EP ECON adopted its position **June 23, 2026 (43-14)** — Euronews framed
  it as backing the digital euro "to reduce US dominance in payments"; adoption aimed
  by end-2026, pilot mid-2027, first issuance possible 2029.
- MiCA's Article 23/58(3) caps (1M transactions + €200M/day as means of exchange →
  halt issuance) are the clearest statutory expression of currency-substitution
  defense. Internal split: Banque de France's Beau backs a private tokenized-euro
  push (Qivalis consortium) over pure defense.

## 3. The global chessboard

- **BIS** AER 2026: stablecoins **fail the three tests of money — singleness,
  elasticity, integrity** — trade like ETFs, pose currency-substitution risks to EMs;
  the tokenized "unified ledger" is the official alternative. WP 1270 models
  safe-asset price effects.
- **IMF** (Dec 2025, "Understanding Stablecoins"): stablecoin cross-border flows now
  exceed BTC/ETH; USD stablecoins in high-inflation EMs accelerate "cryptoization,"
  weaken monetary transmission, erode deposit bases (esp. Africa).
- **China:** mainland ban + e-CNY + mBridge; **Hong Kong Stablecoin Ordinance
  effective Aug 1, 2025** (HKMA licensing, covers CNH); Ant/JD.com offshore yuan
  stablecoin plans **reportedly paused by the PBoC** (late 2025 — media reports only);
  HK proceeded with licensing (Feb 2026).
- **UK:** BoE proposed holding caps (£20k individuals / £10M businesses) — itself a
  defensive cap. **Japan:** JPYC, first yen stablecoin, Oct 27, 2025. **Singapore:**
  MAS single-currency framework, legislation expected mid-2026.
- **Network-effects argument:** payments are winner-take-most; whichever currency
  gets a credible regulated digital rail first entrenches itself (GIS Reports; WEF).

## 4. Critical / skeptical views

- **Recycled-dollars critique** (Kansas City Fed): stablecoins increase Treasury
  demand "only by reducing demand for other assets" — inflows come from MMFs, bank
  deposits, existing offshore dollar demand; one estimate puts $1.5T of growth at
  only ~$200B/yr incremental T-bill demand. The GENIUS interest ban blunts migration
  from yield-bearing MMFs. (Also: Econlib "An Econ 101 Error"; Independent Institute.)
- **Run/fragility:** USDC depegged to $0.87 in March 2023 (SVB); Tether never fully
  audited (2021 CFTC/NYAG: fully backed only 27.6% of the time in 2016-18); a
  fire-sale of a $100B+ T-bill stack is itself a Treasury-market stability risk
  (Fed FEDS note Dec 2025; BPI; S&P).
- **BIS singleness critique:** varying secondary-market prices violate the
  "no-questions-asked" par principle — stablecoins would need central-bank backstops
  to truly be money, so the dominance thesis may overstate their moneyness.
- **Seigniorage offset:** T-bill demand gains may be offset by lost physical-currency
  seigniorage and bank balance-sheet contraction (Vanderbilt Law; KC Fed).
- **Euro-side skeptics:** the digital euro is domestic-payments-focused with tight
  holding limits — it may not counter dollar stablecoins abroad at all; "euro
  stablecoins are scaling while the digital euro waits on Brussels" (Forbes, Jun 2026).

## Flagged / conflicting figures

USD share 97% vs 99%+ (methodology); mid-2026 market cap $290B vs $314-321B;
Tether's $141B UST exposure self-reported; Nigeria figures secondhand; PBoC pause
unofficial; Bessent's $2T (2028) vs $3.7T (2030) — different horizons, both real.
