# Citizenship Application Acceptance Rates — European Union (27 Member States)

*A research report on the acceptance / approval of citizenship (naturalisation) applications across all EU member states, with Malta and Italy highlighted.*

**Compiled:** July 2026 · **Latest data year:** 2024 (Eurostat release of 27 March 2026), with 2023 used where 2024 is not yet broken down by country.

---

## ⚠️ Read this first: what an "acceptance rate" actually means here

There is a critical data-availability point that shapes this entire report:

- **Eurostat and almost every national statistics office publish *grants* (acquisitions of citizenship) — not applications and not rejections.** So for most countries you can see how many people *received* citizenship in a year, but **not** how many *applied* or were *refused*.
- A **true application acceptance rate** = grants ÷ applications (or `1 − rejection rate`). **This is not published by Eurostat, and only a handful of EU countries publish it at all.**
- Eurostat *does* publish a **"naturalisation rate"**, but this is **not** an acceptance rate. It is defined as **citizenships granted in a year ÷ the resident non-national population at the start of that year (×100)** — a propensity/frequency indicator, not an application-outcome ratio.

**Bottom line:** across the EU you can rigorously compare *grants* and the *naturalisation rate*. A genuine EU-wide "acceptance rate" **cannot** be sourced from Eurostat, OECD, EMN, or GLOBALCIT — it only exists, with caveats, in a few national datasets.

### Acceptance rate — applications vs. accepted vs. rejected, by country

This is the table most people actually want: of the people who **applied**, what share were **accepted**. It can only be built where a country publishes both a numerator (grants) and a denominator (applications or total decisions). Where it does, here are the real figures.

### 🇲🇹 Malta — the numbers the statistics office omits (from parliamentary questions & the CBI regulator)

Malta's NSO reports only grants, but Home Affairs ministers have disclosed **refusals** in answers to parliamentary questions (PQs), and the citizenship-by-investment regulator publishes full received/approved/rejected tables. These reveal a picture the grant-only data completely hides:

**Ordinary naturalisation (residence-based) — from PQs:**

The most recent and authoritative figure comes from an **October 2025 parliamentary reply** by Home Affairs Minister Byron Camilleri (to MP Graziella Attard Previ):

> **2,752 people were denied Maltese citizenship by naturalisation over 2020–2024** — of whom **756 were dependants.** *(Lovin Malta, Oct 2025)*

| Year(s) | Granted | Refused | Implied acceptance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2020–2024 (5-yr total)** | — | **2,752 refused** (756 dependants) | see note | **Lovin Malta (Attard Previ PQ, Oct 2025)** — most recent |
| 2022 (only year with both sides) | 339 | **1,372** | **~20%** | MaltaToday (Buttigieg PQ, Jan 2023) |
| 2021 | 421 | — | — | MaltaToday (Buttigieg PQ, Jan 2023) |
| 2023 (all routes) | 2,511 acquisitions | — | — | Malta Independent (Galea PQ, Jan 2024) |

**Reading the numbers:** the 2025 PQ confirms refusals ran at roughly **550/year on average across 2020–2024** — very high against ordinary-naturalisation grants that were only in the low hundreds (421 in 2021, 339 in 2022). In **2022, the one year with both figures, refusals (1,372) outnumbered grants (339) by four to one — an acceptance rate of only ~20%.** Two honest limits: (1) the minister released only the five-year refusal *total*, with no year-by-year split and no matching five-year grant total, so a clean five-year acceptance % cannot be computed — the ~20% is the 2022 single year; (2) grants and refusals may be different-timing cohorts, and refusals can include incomplete files (the minister noted a refusal "had no effect on immigration status"). The raw PQ text on parlament.mt would be needed for the year-by-year split.

## EU-wide headline figures (2024)

- **~1,177,232 people** acquired citizenship of their EU country of residence — **+11.6%** vs 2023.
- **88.0%** were former non-EU nationals; **10.6%** were citizens of another EU country.
- **EU-average naturalisation rate: 2.7** per 100 resident non-nationals.
- **Top granting countries:** Germany 288,700 (24.5%) · Spain 252,500 (21.4%) · **Italy 217,400 (18.5%)** · France 103,700 (8.8%) · Sweden 63,000 (5.4%). The top 5 ≈ **78%** of the EU total.
- **Top former nationalities of new EU citizens:** Syria 110,100 · Morocco 97,100 · Albania 48,000.

**Three-year trend:** 2022 ≈ 0.99M → 2023 = 1.05M (+6.1%) → 2024 = 1.18M (+11.6%). Rising every year.

**Naturalisation-rate range (2024, per 100 non-nationals):** Highest **Sweden 7.5**; Italy 4.1; Spain 3.9; Netherlands 3.9. Lowest **Lithuania 0.1**; Bulgaria 0.3; Estonia 0.3.

## ⭐ Spotlight: Malta and Italy

### 🇲🇹 Malta
- **Grants:** 2,511 (2023); Eurostat reports a **+38.8% rise in 2024** (~3,400 implied — one of the EU's largest increases).
- **Naturalisation rate:** **0.8** per 100 (2022) — among the **lowest** in the EU.
- **Acceptance rate:** **Not published.** Ordinary naturalisation is fully discretionary — the Minister gives no reasons and there is no appeal — so intake and rejection counts are not released.
- **Residence requirement:** ~5 years (12 months continuous immediately before applying + 4 of the preceding 6), plus language/good-character checks.
- **Notable context — the "golden passport":** Malta's citizenship-by-investment scheme (MEIN, 2020) was **struck down by the CJEU Grand Chamber on 29 April 2025** (*Commission v Malta*, C-181/23) as an unlawful "commercialisation" of EU citizenship. New investor applications stopped after the ruling; Malta is reworking a merit-based scheme.

### 🇮🇹 Italy
- **Grants:** 213,567 (2023) → ~217,400 (2024) — **2nd–3rd largest in the EU.**
- **Naturalisation rate:** **4.1** per 100 (2023 and 2024) — **2nd–3rd highest** in the EU.
- **Acceptance rate:** **Not published.** (The "4.1" is a naturalisation rate, not an acceptance rate.) Statutory decision deadline is up to 24–48 months.
- **Residence requirement:** **10 years** for non-EU nationals (4 for EU, 5 for refugees, 2 by marriage). B1 Italian + income + clean record.
- **Composition caveat:** Italy's totals are *not* mostly 10-year residence naturalisation — 2024 was ~42% residence, ~32% transmission to minors, plus marriage and acquisition-at-18 routes.
- **Notable context — jus sanguinis reform:** Italy historically had *no generational limit* on citizenship by descent, creating huge consular backlogs. **Decree-Law 36/2025** (law of 24 May 2025) introduced a **two-generation limit** (parent or grandparent born in Italy).

## Regional detail

### Southern Europe

**🇪🇸 Spain** — Largest grant volume in the EU in 2023 (240,208) and 2nd in 2024 (252,500). Uniquely, Spain publishes **denials**, so an acceptance rate is *approximable*: denials surged from ~7,400 (2023) to **20,099 (2024)**. Against ~258,000 files resolved in 2023 that implies ~97% acceptance among resolved files, but rejections are rising sharply. Residence: 10 years general, but only **2 years** for Ibero-American, Portuguese, Andorran, Filipino, Equatorial Guinean nationals and Sephardic Jews — which drives Spain's high volumes.

**🇵🇹 Portugal** — Three non-comparable "grant" counts: IRN approvals all routes (~220,000 in 2024), INE acquisitions incl. people living abroad (~46,700 in 2024, only ~15–17k resident in Portugal), and Eurostat resident basis (~17–20k/yr). The surge was driven by the **Sephardic Jews route** (now closed) and descent claims — *not* resident-immigrant naturalisation. A **2025–26 reform raises ordinary residence from 5 to 10 years** (7 for EU/CPLP nationals), in force 19 May 2026.

**🇬🇷 Greece** — The **only country with a clean published decision-level acceptance rate**: for ordinary naturalisation, **71.3% positive** in 2023 (3,515 of 4,931 decisions), and **97.3%** for the second-generation route. Note this is decisions, not applications-submitted (backlog 30,000+, ~1,494-day average processing). A citizenship exam (introduced 2021) had a **~50.7% pass rate** in the April/May 2025 sitting.

**🇨🇾 Cyprus** — Grants fell from 3,318 (2023) to 2,800 (2024); naturalisation rate **1.21 (2024)**, below EU average. Cyprus's historical prominence was in *per-capita* grants inflated by the **Cyprus Investment Programme** (6,779 citizenships 2007–2020), which was **terminated in Nov 2020** after the "Cyprus Papers"; 304 people later had citizenship revoked. Residence tightened to 7 years in 2023.

### Western Europe

**🇫🇷 France** — 103,661 acquisitions in 2024 (48,494 naturalisations by decree). France decides at three levels (favourable / adjournment / rejection) but does **not** release a consolidated national applications-and-rejection table, so no published acceptance rate. Residence: 5 years.

**🇩🇪 Germany** — Record 291,955 grants in 2024 (+46%). Administered by Länder/local authorities, so no national acceptance rate. **2024 reform** (effective 27 June 2024) cut ordinary residence from **8 → 5 years** and generally permitted dual citizenship; the 3-year fast track was repealed in October 2025.

**🇳🇱 Netherlands** — Best decision-level data in the EU: 43,930 applications, 53,590 decisions, **97% positive** (2023). Residence: 5 years (3 for spouses).

**🇧🇪 Belgium** — 60,108 grants in 2024, highest in ~two decades. Formal "naturalisation" is now marginal (only ~0.4% of cases); the dominant route is **declaration of nationality**, so an acceptance rate for naturalisation proper is not meaningful. Residence: 5 years.

**🇱🇺 Luxembourg** — 7,415 grants in 2024 (down from 11,904 as the descent-based "recovery" route winds down), but the **highest per-capita rate in the EU (8.8 per 1,000 population)**. Residence: 5 years.

**🇦🇹 Austria** — 21,891 grants in 2024. Among the EU's strictest: **10 years** ordinary residence (6 with proven integration), and dual citizenship generally not permitted for naturalisation. No acceptance rate published.

**🇮🇪 Ireland** — Record >30,000 decisions in 2024 from ~31,000 applications processed (grants ≈ decisions, near-total approval), median processing cut to 8 months. Fully digital since October 2023. Residence: 5 of the last 9 years.

### Nordics & Baltics

**🇸🇪 Sweden** — ~67,800 grants (2023); **highest naturalisation rate in the EU every year** (10.6 → 7.9 → 7.5 across 2022–24). ~58,000 citizenship applications in 2023. Historically no language/civics test (a test is planned ~2027–28). No published acceptance rate; long processing backlogs flagged by the national audit office. Residence: 5 years.

**🇩🇰 Denmark** — 6,255 grants in 2024, up 86% from 2023 — the **largest relative increase of any EU country**. Citizenship is conferred only by **Act of Parliament** (twice-yearly bills), so grants cluster by legislative cycle. Among the EU's strictest (B2 Danish, citizenship test, no public debt, handshake ceremony). Residence: 9 years.

**🇫🇮 Finland** — 11,512 grants (2024); 17,063 applications. Migri publishes decision-level data, but only an all-permit-types aggregate (**84% positive**, 2024) — not citizenship-specific. **Residence raised from 5 to 8 years, effective 1 October 2024.**

**🇪🇪 Estonia** — Low hundreds of grants/year and falling (one of the EU's largest decreases in 2024); naturalisation rate **0.3 (2024)**. Legacy of a large **"persons with undetermined citizenship"** population (~65,000, ~6% in 2022, down from 32% in 1992). Residence: 8 years + language and constitution exams.

**🇱🇻 Latvia** — 694 naturalisations (2024); naturalisation rate **0.4**. Distinct **"non-citizen" (nepilsoņi)** population (~169,000, ~9% in 2024). Since 1 Jan 2020 children of non-citizens are citizens at birth, so the pool shrinks. Residence: 10 years + exams.

**🇱🇹 Lithuania** — Only **90 naturalisations in 2024**; naturalisation rate **0.1 — the lowest in the EU.** Lithuania generally does not permit dual citizenship for naturalising adults, and took the "zero option" at independence, so it has no large stateless legacy. Residence: 10 years.

### Central & Eastern Europe

**🇵🇱 Poland** — 13,900 grants (2023, +23.3%). President's discretionary grant has no fixed criteria and no appeal, so no acceptance statistics. Residence: 3 years on permanent residence (1 year for Polish-origin / Card of the Pole).

**🇨🇿 Czechia** — ~4,500 grants (2023); rate ~0.8 (among EU's lowest). A "~85% approval" figure appears only in a commercial blog — **not** an official rate. Tax/social-security debts are a leading rejection ground. Residence: 5 years permanent (~10 effective).

**🇸🇰 Slovakia** — Grants rose 59.2% in 2023 but remain in the EU's lowest tier. Residence: **8 years** permanent residence. No acceptance rate published.

**🇭🇺 Hungary** — 2024 grants fell 28.3%; dominated by ethnic-Hungarian co-ethnics (71% Romanian, 20% Slovak nationals). **Simplified naturalisation (2011): no residency required** for ethnic Hungarians — **>1.1 million granted since 2011.** Ordinary route: 8 years.

**🇸🇮 Slovenia** — ~1,463 grants (2022). Residence: 10 years (5 continuous before application). No acceptance rate published.

**🇭🇷 Croatia** — Highest EU share of female recipients (62.2%) and highest median age (40.4) in 2023. **Article 16: no residence required** for ethnic Croats (heavily used by Bosnian Croats). Ordinary: 8 years continuous.

**🇷🇴 Romania** — Naturalisation rate **26.8 (2022, highest in EU)** / 5.9 (2023) — **inflated** because grants overwhelmingly go to **non-resident Moldovans** reacquiring citizenship (Art. 11 restoration; ~850k total by 2024; >150,000 applications pending). 2024 grants fell 67.9% — the largest EU decrease. Ordinary residence: 8 years.

**🇧🇬 Bulgaria** — Grants rose from 12,205 (2023) to 16,000+ (2024); naturalisation rate **0.3 (2024)**, among EU's lowest. Top origins reflect the **Bulgarian-origin simplified track** (N. Macedonia, Moldova, Albania, Ukraine, Türkiye). Ordinary residence: 5 years.

## Key takeaways

1. **A true "acceptance rate" barely exists as comparable EU data.** Only the **Netherlands (97%)** and **Greece (71.3% ordinary)** publish a clean decision-level positive share. **Spain** publishes denials (approximable), and **Ireland/Finland** publish partial decision data. All other member states report **grant counts only**.
2. **Use the naturalisation rate for cross-country comparison** — but remember it measures grants against the resident-foreigner *stock*, not against applications, and is distorted for diaspora-heavy countries (Romania, Hungary, Croatia, Bulgaria).
3. **"Grants" ≠ residence-based naturalisation.** Italy's totals are heavily transmission/marriage; Portugal's are heavily descent/Sephardic; Greece's are ~50% second-generation; Belgium's are mostly by declaration. Comparing raw totals across countries without decomposition is misleading.
4. **Rates are converging downward via reform.** Germany eased to 5 years (2024) while Finland (5→8), Portugal (5→10), Austria (10) and the Baltics (8–10) remain strict — several tightening further in 2025–26.
5. **Investment-citizenship is closing.** Malta's scheme was struck down by the CJEU (April 2025) and Cyprus terminated its programme (2020) — both distorted historical figures.

### Data caveats

- **Eurostat reports grants, not applications or rejections** — a genuine application acceptance rate is not available at EU level (see the framing section above).
- The research environment's outbound proxy blocked direct fetches to `ec.europa.eu` and several national statistics sites, so Eurostat cell values here rest on its news releases and national/press reproductions citing `migr_acq`. Figures flagged *(est.)*, *(uncertain)* or *n/c* — notably exact 2023–24 naturalisation rates and grant counts for Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Croatia, Denmark and Finland, and Portugal's exact 2024 rate — should be confirmed directly against the `migr_acq` table if unit-level precision is required.
- Metrics are **not all the same denominator**: most naturalisation rates are per 100 resident non-nationals, but Belgium, Luxembourg and Ireland figures shown are per 1,000 total population (marked *‰ pop*).
