Multichannel Campaign: the outreach engine that lives inside HubSpot and speaks every channel Web3 uses.

Give it a company or a list. It warms and reaches each prospect across email, Telegram, Discord, X and LinkedIn — driven by a supervised AI agent, logged back to HubSpot. This brief covers the product, the features, and every vendor doing something close.

01

Product description

What it is, who it's for, and why it exists as a HubSpot add-on rather than yet another standalone platform.

Positioning

The only multichannel campaign engine that is HubSpot-native, Web3-first, and agent-driven. Web3 teams already keep HubSpot as their system of record. Today they bolt on a standalone Telegram tool, leave the CRM to run Discord and X by hand, and get no help warming a prospect before the pitch. Multichannel Campaign turns HubSpot itself into the command center: one play, five channels, one supervised AI agent doing the work.

The one job

Given a company — or a whole list — run a coordinated, personalized play across every channel a Web3 buyer actually uses, and warm them before you ask for anything.

Who it's for

Growth, BD and community leads at the ~2,000 Web3-native companies already on HubSpot. Small teams who can't hire an SDR per channel and won't rip out their CRM to get Telegram.

Why an add-on, not a platform

The Web3 leaders (Spredo, Enreach, CRMChat) make you leave HubSpot. We layer onto it — no migration, no second source of truth, install-and-go from the marketplace.

What makes it defensible

Web3 channel depth (incl. Discord) + engagement warming + a supervised agent, all native to HubSpot. No competitor combines all four (see §03).

02

Feature description

Six feature groups. The engine that makes them work — and the reason this is a real build, not a macro — is the AI agent orchestrator in the middle.

F1

Audience & input

Start from one company, a HubSpot list, a saved segment, or a signal (a raise, a ship event). The agent resolves each account into the real people — and their wallets, Telegram / Discord / X / GitHub handles — before anything sends.

  • Company, list, segment or signal as input
  • Auto-resolves contacts & handles (via Web3 Enrich)
  • Dedupes against what's already in HubSpot
F2

Channel action catalog

Not just messaging — the follows, likes, connects and reactions that warm a prospect first. Warming actions are marked amber.

✉ Email
  • send
  • sequence / follow-up
✈ Telegram
  • DM · group message
  • follow / join
❖ Discord
  • DM · server post
  • join · react
𝕏 X
  • reply / DM
  • follow · like
in LinkedIn
  • message
  • connect · like

amber = engagement / warming action (ToS-sensitive — see §04).

F3

AI agent orchestrator — the core engine

required, not optional

Every action above is decided and carried out by an agent, not a static if-then flow. Choosing who to touch, on which channel, in what order; writing each message in context; timing a like before a DM; reading the reply and picking the next step — that is agentic work. This is the difference between a campaign tool and a sequence macro, and it's precisely what we already build.

Planner
Builds the play per target: channel order, warming steps, timing, message intent.
→
Channel agents
Per-channel executors (email · TG · Discord · X · LinkedIn) run steps within guardrails.
→
Supervisor gate
Routes anything sensitive to a human for one-click approve / edit / skip.
→
Reply handler
Reads responses, updates HubSpot, decides the next move or hands off to a rep.
  • Human-in-the-loop by default — mirrors the market's proven-safe posture (Spredo, Enreach, CRMChat all run supervised agents; consensus is hybrid > fully autonomous)
  • Guardrails per channel: rate limits, warm-up, sending-account rotation, quiet hours
  • Runs 24/7 but never sends anything a human hasn't set a policy for
F4

Personalization & signal triggers

Each touch is written from the contact's real context — chain, TVL, last raise, recent GitHub ship, the group they're in — and campaigns can fire off a signal instead of a manual push.

  • On-chain & social context injected per message
  • Triggered by Raise Radar / Ship Signal / Risk Trigger
  • A/B variants, tone control, language
F5

HubSpot-native integration

HubSpot stays the system of record. Every touch, reply and outcome writes to the contact timeline; campaigns enroll from HubSpot lists and workflows.

  • Custom objects/properties for handles & channels
  • Two-way sync · workflow enrollment · timeline logging
  • Marketplace-app packaging (displaces NisWire)
F6

Safety & compliance layer

The one thing that decides whether this is a growth tool or a banned-account generator. Built in from day one, not bolted on: per-account rate limits and warm-up, sending-account rotation, opt-out capture across every channel, and a hard human-approval gate on the highest-risk actions (LinkedIn connects, X/Telegram/Discord mass DM). Engagement automation is sold as assisted, not fully autonomous.

The white space

No vendor combines HubSpot-native + Web3 + all five channels + engagement warming + a supervised AI agent. The Web3 leaders — Spredo, Enreach, CRMChat — are strong on Telegram and AI but are standalone platforms that ask you to leave HubSpot, and all three are weak-to-absent on Discord and on LinkedIn/X engagement actions. The only HubSpot-native option, NisWire, is Telegram-only with no agent and no warming. The best engagement engine, La Growth Machine, has zero Web3 channels (no Telegram, no Discord). Discord outbound is barely served by anyone — a differentiator and the biggest ToS risk at once.

Two honest reads. (1) The AI agent is now table stakes — everyone has one; our edge is channel breadth + HubSpot-native + Web3 depth, with the agent as the delivery mechanism, not the pitch. (2) The reason no one does "all five channels + warming" may partly be that Discord/X/LinkedIn engagement automation is ToS-risky — so leading with it is both our opening and our exposure.

04

Inside their AI SDR agents

"AI agent" is the column everyone claims. Here's what those agents actually do — the shared capability stack, and the two moats we don't have yet.

1 · Discover
Build & refresh a contact DB — scrape Telegram groups, aggregators, LinkedIn.
→
2 · Research
Pull per-prospect context to personalize.
→
3 · Write
Generate first-touch + follow-ups, trained on domain conversations.
→
4 · Send
Multi-account, rate-managed, across channels.
→
5 · Qualify
Answer FAQs, ask discovery questions, assess fit.
→
6 · Route & book
Escalate hot leads, book meetings via calendar.

Spredo · Web3

AI SDR (beta) answers FAQs & pre-qualifies before escalating to a human. A proprietary algorithm scrapes 300+ curated Telegram groups (incl. private conference groups) + LinkedIn into a live Web3 DB with 30+ filters. Supervised, with per-campaign account governance. Ships a Claude MCP — Claude itself selects contacts, builds campaigns and runs outreach.

Enreach · Web3

Agents trained on 30M sales conversations + 25M historical Telegram messages — a domain-tuned model. Auto-engages inbound, asks discovery questions, assesses fit, routes hot leads; runs follow-up sequences + calendar booking; up to 12 TG accounts / 5k msgs/mo; a 500M-lead database.

11x · Alice (general)

Autonomous end-to-end: research → write → send across email, LinkedIn, SMS, WhatsApp and voice (with Julian); reply handling, CRM updates, meeting booking, multilingual. Positioned to replace headcount, minimal oversight. Not Web3.

Artisan · Ava (general)

Email + LinkedIn SDR: finds & researches prospects, writes personalized emails, sends follow-ups at scale. Lighter than 11x on channels, CRM breadth and orchestration. Not Web3.

Two philosophies

Supervised / hybrid (Spredo, Enreach, Artisan) — a human refines, brand-safe; the Web3 default.  vs  Autonomous (11x Alice) — minimal oversight, replace headcount. For Web3's pseudonymous, community-sensitive buyers, supervised is winning.

What this means for us

The capability stack above is table stakes — we must match it. The two moats we don't have: Enreach's Web3-conversation-trained model, and the big proprietary contact DBs (Spredo 300k, Enreach 500M). We'd lean on enrichment + the customer's own HubSpot data — so "own vs partner for a Web3 dataset" is a real decision. Upside: Spredo shipping a Claude MCP proves the agent-orchestrator direction — and that's the exact stack we already run on.
