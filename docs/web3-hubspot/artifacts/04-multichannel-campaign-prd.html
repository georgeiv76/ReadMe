Summary & strategy

Multichannel Campaign is a HubSpot Marketplace add-on that lets a Web3 growth/BD team launch one coordinated outreach play across email, Telegram and X — including the warming actions (follow, like, join) that precede a cold message — with an AI agent doing the work under human supervision, and every touch logged back to HubSpot as the system of record.

The strategy, decided in research, is deliberately narrow:

  • Positioning — "stay on HubSpot." We do not compete with standalone Web3 platforms (Spredo, Enreach) on their turf. We distribute through the HubSpot Marketplace to the ~2,000 Web3-native companies already on HubSpot, and displace the weak incumbent there (NisWire — Telegram-only, no agent, no warming).
  • Data — enrichment, not a database. Fuel is the customer's own HubSpot data, enriched on demand (Clay / Apollo / DeFiLlama). We do not build a proprietary contact DB — that is the one race the incumbents already won.
  • Agent — supervised. Human-in-the-loop by default, matching the Web3-winning posture. Warming/engagement actions ship as assisted, never fully autonomous.
One-line pitch: "Run email + Telegram + X campaigns from inside HubSpot — warmed, personalized, and supervised — without leaving your CRM."

Goals & non-goals

In scope (v1)

  • HubSpot Marketplace app, OAuth-installed
  • Campaigns across email, Telegram, X
  • Warming actions: X follow/like, TG join
  • On-demand enrichment of HubSpot contacts
  • Supervised AI agent (plan → act → reply) with approval queue
  • Two-way HubSpot sync + timeline logging
  • Per-account rate limits, warm-up, opt-out
  • Unified reply inbox + basic reporting

Explicitly deferred

  • Discord & LinkedIn channels → phase 2
  • Proprietary Web3 contact database
  • Conversation-trained (fine-tuned) model
  • Fully-autonomous (no-human) sending
  • On-chain attribution (Proof Loop add-on)
  • Voice / SMS / WhatsApp channels

Primary goal: a Web3 growth lead can launch a warmed, personalized, 3-channel campaign to a HubSpot list in under 15 minutes, approve each sensitive touch in one click, and see every interaction in HubSpot — with zero account bans.

3

Target user & ICP

Buyer & user: Head of Growth / BD / Community at a Web3-native company, 11–200 employees, already using HubSpot. Small team, no SDR-per-channel budget, marketing/community-led motion, buyers live on Telegram and X.

Market: ~2,000 Web3-native accounts on HubSpot (from sizing research). Beachhead = teams currently forcing NisWire or manual Telegram/X outreach.

Pricing posture (for context, not this PRD's scope): self-serve, impulse-grade ($/seat or $/sending-account per month), with a free/cheap tier as the marketplace front door.

User stories

  • Launch — "As a growth lead, I select a HubSpot list of target companies and launch a campaign that touches each contact on email, Telegram and X, so I reach Web3 buyers where they respond."
  • Warm — "I want the agent to follow and like a prospect on X and join their Telegram before it DMs, so cold outreach converts instead of getting ignored."
  • Supervise — "I review AI-drafted touches in an approval queue and approve/edit/skip in one click, so I stay brand-safe and never trip a ban."
  • Personalize — "Each message is written from the contact's on-chain and social context, so it doesn't read like a blast."
  • Trigger — "A campaign can auto-start when a target raises or ships, so I reach people at the right moment."
  • Reply — "Replies land in one inbox and are logged to the HubSpot timeline; hot leads are routed to me."
  • Trust — "HubSpot stays my source of truth — nothing lives in a second CRM."

Agent architecture

A supervised multi-agent loop. HubSpot is the record; the orchestration service is the brain; connectors are the hands.

HubSpot
Source of truth: contacts, lists, timeline, workflows.
→
Enrichment
Clay · Apollo · DeFiLlama adapters resolve handles + context.
→
Planner
Builds the per-target play.
→
Supervisor
Human approval queue on sensitive steps.
→
Executors
Email · TG · X agents act within guardrails.
→
Reply handler
Ingests replies, logs to HubSpot, decides next step.
Note on our stack: this is orchestration we already run — and Spredo shipping a Claude MCP for the same job validates the approach. The build is the connectors + guardrails + HubSpot glue, not the agent concept.
8

HubSpot data model & sync

Minimum custom schema (names indicative):

  • Contact properties — telegram_handle, x_handle, wallet, ens, chains, last_raise, icp_score, opt_out_channels.
  • Custom object: Sending Account — channel, identifier, health status, daily caps, warm-up stage.
  • Custom object: Campaign — goal, audience source, channel plan, bound accounts, state.
  • Custom object: Touch — contact, channel, action type, status, timestamp, approver — mirrored to the contact timeline as an activity.

Sync is two-way; the add-on never becomes a second CRM — it reads lists/segments and writes activities, notes and tasks back.

Key screens

  • Campaign builder — pick audience → compose the play (channels, warming, timing) → set accounts & guardrails → launch.
  • Approval queue — the daily driver: pending touches with full context, one-click approve/edit/skip, bulk actions.
  • Unified inbox — replies across email/TG/X, threaded, HubSpot-linked, with agent-suggested responses.
  • Account health — sending assets, warm-up progress, rate-limit consumption, flags.
  • HubSpot contact card — the add-on's touches/replies inline on the native record.

Safety & compliance

The feature that decides whether this is a growth tool or a banned-account generator. Non-negotiable for v1.

  • Email — CAN-SPAM / GDPR: unsubscribe in every email, sender identity, suppression list, sending-domain warm-up.
  • Telegram — anti-spam bans are aggressive: phone-number accounts, gradual warm-up, per-account daily caps, rotation, no unsolicited bulk blasts.
  • X — automation policy + follow/like/DM caps: throttled warming, human-approved DMs, per-account limits.
  • Global — warming + all cold messages pass the human approval gate; opt-outs suppress across channels; audit log of who approved what.
Product principle: engagement automation is sold as assisted, not autonomous. The approval gate is a feature, not a limitation — it is what keeps customer accounts alive and the brand safe. This also matches the Web3 market's proven-safe (supervised) posture.
11

Non-functional requirements

  • Deliverability & reliability — queue-based execution, retries, no message loss; graceful degradation if a channel API is down.
  • Security — encrypted credential/token storage per connected account; least-privilege HubSpot scopes; SOC2-track practices.
  • Privacy — GDPR-aware (EU prospecting flag), data retention controls, per-contact opt-out honored everywhere.
  • Scalability — concurrent campaigns across many accounts; rate-limit accounting per account/channel.
  • Observability — full audit trail of agent decisions + human approvals; cost tracking (LLM + enrichment credits).

Risks & open questions

  • ToS / ban liability — even scoped to email+TG+X, warming is risky. Mitigation is the safety layer; open question: how much liability sits with us vs the customer's own accounts.
  • Email infrastructure — send via HubSpot's own email, the customer's ESP, or our own sending stack? Affects deliverability ownership.
  • Telegram account sourcing — customers bring their own numbers, or we provision? Warm-up time is a real onboarding cost.
  • X API — cost tier and automation-policy limits may constrain warming volume; validate before committing.
  • Enrichment + LLM unit economics — per-contact credit + token cost must fit an impulse-grade price.
  • HubSpot Marketplace requirements — review/security bar and custom-object limits on lower HubSpot tiers.
  • Cold-start — without a contact DB, value depends on the customer's existing HubSpot data; may need a light sourcing path (the Stack Finder add-on) sooner than planned.
