Eleven single-purpose add-ons that make HubSpot speak Web3.

Web3 teams keep HubSpot as their system of record — but it can't see wallets, Telegram, GitHub, or the moment a protocol ships. We already run the orchestrators that fill every one of those gaps. This turns them into a line of cheap, one-problem-each add-ons.

Why many small add-ons, not one suite. A buyer approving a $30–80/mo tool decides in minutes on a single, obvious pain — not on a platform migration. One problem per add-on keeps the pitch legible, the trial short, and the "yes" cheap. Land one, expand into the rest.

Telegram Hub
telegram
Everything Telegram, in one place — the channel HubSpot can't touch and Web3 lives on.

Merges all Telegram activity into one engine: logs inbound DMs and group chats to HubSpot, and sends outbound — DM a user, message a group, follow or join — with handle resolution and multi-account management. It's the engine every campaign calls for its Telegram steps.

built from → tg-finder tg-hubspot-backfill
closes: channel gapeffort: med
Discord Hub
discord
Discord is where Web3 communities actually live — servers, roles, mods — and HubSpot is completely blind to it.

The Discord counterpart to Telegram Hub: logs inbound DMs and mentions into HubSpot, and sends outbound — DM a member, post to a server channel, join, react — with member resolution and bot-account management. Powers the Discord steps of any campaign.

built from → web3-osint new build · patterns Telegram Hub
closes: channel gapeffort: high · net-new
Proof Loop
attribution
HubSpot's cookie tracking breaks on wallets and cross-device — you can't prove what a campaign actually drove.

Wallet-aware, cookieless journey tracking that ties a campaign touch to an on-chain action — a mint, a stake, a bridge — and writes the outcome back onto the HubSpot contact. Turns "opens and clicks" into "wallets acquired."

built from → dedaub-gtm traffic-investigation ga-gsc-gtm-report
closes: attribution gapeffort: high
Reality check on the engagement actions. Auto-follow, auto-like, auto-connect and bulk DM run against each platform's terms — LinkedIn and X in particular detect and ban aggressive automation, and Telegram and Discord will kill spamming bots fast. This is buildable and sellable, but only with human-in-the-loop approval, per-account rate limits, warm-up and account rotation built in from day one. Sell "likes & connects at scale" as assisted, not fully autonomous — that's the line between a growth tool and a burned account.

04

Sequencing — what to ship, in what order

Lead with what's already built and universally painful. Earn the install, then expand across the funnel. Save the moat for when it can be done right.

PHASE 1 · LAND

Get installed

Lowest effort, broadest pain, already running.

  • CRM Cleaner (free wedge)
  • Web3 Enrich
  • Telegram Hub
PHASE 2 · VALUE

Generate leads

Turn the install into pipeline they can measure.

  • Raise Radar
  • Stack Finder
  • ICP Score
PHASE 3 · ACTIVATE

Close the loop

Reach and prove — higher build, higher stickiness.

  • Multichannel Campaign
  • Discord Hub
  • Ship Signal
PHASE 4 · MOAT

Own the category

The parts only Dedaub can build. Premium tier.

  • Risk Trigger
  • Proof Loop

Pricing posture

Price each add-on as a self-serve, impulse-grade purchase — the buyer says yes on one obvious pain, not a platform decision. Cross-sell the funnel once they trust one. Reserve premium pricing for the moat, where there's no substitute.

Two open decisions before this becomes a build plan.  (1) Buyer vs. pipeline — is Risk Trigger a customer feature, or Dedaub's own lead engine? That changes whether it's packaged or kept internal. (2) Build vs. wrap — own the enrichment data (defensible, slower) or orchestrate existing providers and focus the build on signals + the HubSpot glue (faster, cheaper).  Both also flagged in chat.
